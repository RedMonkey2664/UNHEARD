# 01 - Game design

## 1. Vision

UNHEARD is a 20-minute game you play in the dark with headphones on. Your
phone is your torch, your audio player and your timer, and it runs on one
battery. You are restoring the voicemails of a grandmother you were always too
busy for, while a family member hunts you for them and a ghost whispers that
you should stop listening.

It is three games braided together:
  - Heart: an emotional listening game about the word "later".
  - Thriller: a stealth game against a predictable human villain.
  - Horror: a slow-dread layer with one temptation that punishes impatience.

## 2. Design pillars

  1. The mechanic IS the emotion. Battery loss feels like time running out.
     Rushing is literally punished. Listening is literally rewarded.
  2. Sound first. Everything important can be heard before it is seen.
  3. Fair fear. The player is never killed by something they could not have
     known. Every threat has a cue and a counter.
  4. One theme: "later". The guilt of saying it, the horror that feeds on it.
  5. Small and finishable. 4 rooms, 1 villain, 1 ghost pair, 1 core loop.

## 3. Audience, tone, content notes

  Audience: Indian college students and young adults. Hinglish voice.
  Tone: warm domestic textures (steel plates, chai, ceiling fan, marigold
  calendar) slowly turning cold and teal. Sad first, scary second.
  Content notes shown on the title screen:
    - Death of a loved one (implied violence, nothing shown)
    - Psychological horror, 3 jump scares
    - Flashing lights (can be reduced in settings)
    - Best played with headphones
  No gore. No on-screen violence. Vikram's crime is implied through audio.

## 4. Story

### Backstory (what really happened)

Nani, an old woman, lived alone in a rented-then-owned flat. Her nephew
Vikram Mehra (Mom's cousin, whom she calls by name and who calls her Maasi)
wanted her to sign the flat over to him. She refused. Over five months she
left the player a series of voicemails, every one a small act of love, and
each one the player answered with "later". Seven are on her phone.

Chronology of the voicemails (the player hears them in this order):
  VM1  Exam Week    ~5 months before. Nani waits outside the door with chai.
  VM2  Birthday     Nani sings off-key. The player cuts the call.
  VM3  Monsoon      Nani gives away her umbrella. Vikram is heard arguing
                    with her in the background.
  VM4  Dal          Nani teaches the recipe "by feel". Mentions the notebook.
  VM5  The Argument The player snapped at her. She apologises. Mentions a note.
  VM6  Hospital     Weak voice. "Don't tell your mother I called." Vikram had
                    visited with papers.
  VM7  The Silent One 11:42 PM, the night she fell. Her phone was dialling the
                    player. 11 seconds: TV noise, breathing, a key in the
                    lock, "Vikram? Itni raat ko?", a thud. The player saw the
                    name on the screen and thought "later".
  VM8  Eat Properly 7:58 PM the same evening, hours before. The last time she
                    ever spoke to the player. Unplayed. 8 seconds.

Vikram pushed her. It was ruled an accident. He knows she was recording
things. He needs her phone before anyone hears the voicemails and her tape
recorder before anyone hears the tape.

### The night of the game

The funeral ended at six. Mom went to Aunt's place (Mausi's) for the night
and texted that she will not be back until tomorrow. You said you would lock
up Nani's flat. It is about 9:40 PM. You find her phone with 7 unplayed
voicemails and a battery that will not last.

### What the player slowly learns

  Act 1: this is a sad, small ghost story. (VM1, VM2)
  Act 2: someone else was there. Vikram arrives with a key. Nani was scared
         of someone at home. (VM3 to VM6)
  Act 3: Mom texts that Vikram is "looking in on you". It was the night she
         fell. You missed her call. (VM7)
  Act 4: open her locker. The code is in her notebook. (Locker)
  Act 5: you have the tape. Survive until the police arrive. (Final act)
  (Acts are derived from completed voicemails, docs/03 section 2: a VM is
  played in the act BEFORE its memory ends.)

## 5. Characters

### The player ("you")
  Gender-neutral college student. Always "too busy". Inner voice appears
  only in memory scenes as short English text. Never speaks aloud.

### Nani (the heart)
  Gentle, humble, apologetic ("Sorry, I talk too much"). Hinglish.
  Rule: she says "beta" AT MOST ONCE per voicemail.
  As a ghost she is helpful and never attacks. She appears as a warm steel
  cup of chai, a figure at the end of a corridor, a lamp that flickers to
  warn you, a reflection a second late.

### Vikram Mehra (the thriller)
  Mom's cousin. Charming, soft-voiced, family man in public. Calls the
  player "beta". Walks the flat calmly while pretending to be helpful
  ("Mummy ne phone mangwaya hai"). Never shouts until the final act.
  Is predictable: fixed patrol, fixed rhythm, learnable.

### The Hollow (the horror)
  A ghost in the static between voicemails. It copies Nani's voice but gets
  the rhythm and the words slightly wrong. It overuses "beta". It wants you
  to rush ("play the last one first"), to stay ("it's warm here") and to stop
  listening. It feeds on the word "later".
  Tells: says "beta" multiple times per line. Teal subtitle colour. Slightly
  wrong cadence. Appears only in sound until Presence is high.

### Mom (Mummy)
  Only as text messages and, in the good ending, a voice at the door.
  She trusts Vikram completely.

## 6. Theme

"Later." Every voicemail is something the player put off. The Hollow's power
is the player's own habit of postponing. The good ending is the player doing
the opposite: staying, listening, eating the meal she asked for.

## 7. Core loop

  Explore rooms (torch)
        |
        v
  Find the clue for the current voicemail (examine objects, pick up tools)
        |
        v
  FIX the voicemail (item, puzzle or sequence)
        |
        v
  PLAY it (battery drains, player cannot move, 11-20 seconds)
        |
        v
  MEMORY scene (20 seconds, Vikram frozen) -> evidence unlocked
        |
        +--> loop to the next voicemail
        
  Underneath, always running:
  Vikram's patrol (noise, sight, hiding)  and  Presence (the Hollow)

## 8. Structure and pacing (target 18 to 22 minutes)

  0:00 - 1:30   Prologue. Headphone gate, arrival text, Mom's text, tutorial
                hints. Vikram is not in the flat. Safe.
  1:30 - 4:30   Act 1. VM1, VM2. Soft horror beats. Foreshadow footsteps.
  4:30 - 12:00  Act 2. Vikram enters (key in the lock). VM3, VM4, VM5, VM6.
                The temptation button appears after VM4.
  12:00 - 14:00 Act 3. Mom's second text. VM7, under the tightest away
                window (pause mid-playback and hide).
  14:00 - 15:00 Act 4. Walk to the locker (15 s away windows, route B).
  15:00 - 19:00 Act 5. Tape, call 112, survive 60 seconds.
  19:00 - 21:00 Ending scene, end card, play again.

Vikram's rhythm in Acts 2 to 4: roughly 60 seconds of patrol, then he steps
outside (to "take a call"). The gap shrinks each act. That gap is when you
fix and play voicemails.

## 9. Motive, constraints, win and fail (precise)

Motive:
  Hear every voicemail, collect proof, open the locker, call the police and
  survive until they arrive.

Win:
  Survive the 75-second police countdown without being caught and without the
  Hollow taking over. Quality of win depends on Presence.

Fail (each is an ending, not a game-over screen):
  - CAUGHT: Vikram reaches you, or noise hits 100 with him close and you are
    not hidden, or battery hits 0 and 35 seconds pass.
  - LATER: you press PLAY #8 NOW, or Presence reaches 100 (cause
    'presence_doom').

Hard constraints (the rules the player feels):
  - One battery shared by torch, playback and call.
  - Voicemails in order. Each needs a fix.
  - A wrong fix costs battery and raises Presence.
  - Skipping a memory scene raises Presence.
  - Noise is only dangerous when Vikram is near.

## 10. Emotional beat map

  VM1  sweet, small guilt ("later")
  VM2  embarrassment, cut call
  VM3  protectiveness, a stranger's voice in the rain
  VM4  tenderness, "andaaz se"
  VM5  the guilt peak: "Sorry, I talk too much" (costs the most battery)
  VM6  dread, the first clear threat
  VM7  devastation: "Later never came."
  VM8  release: "Eat properly, beta."

## 11. Fair-play rules for clues (the designer must obey these)

  1. Every fix has a clue the player can find in the same session without
     leaving the four rooms.
  2. The Hollow's tell (overusing "beta") is audible from the first whisper.
  3. Mom's first text ("Won't be back till tomorrow") is the clue that makes
     the LATER ending land. It must be shown on screen for at least 6 seconds
     and stay readable in the phone MESSAGES tab.
  4. The temptation is always optional and always lies.
  5. Vikram always makes footsteps. Footstep volume and pan must always
     reflect his real position.

## 12. Difficulty

Default: tense but fair. An Assist toggle in settings reduces noise gain by
40%, Vikram speed by 15% and battery costs by 20% (numbers in docs/03).
The first run should take about 20 minutes. A confident second run about 14.

## 13. Out of scope

Multiplayer, save/load, inventory screens beyond the phone NOTES tab,
branching dialogue, procedurally generated rooms, voice input, localisation
beyond Hinglish.
